Build a professional occupational health intelligence web app called “SME Risk Intelligence Engine.”

Purpose:
This app is an internal occupational health decision-support and report-drafting platform for Subject Matter Experts. It should help SMEs evaluate medical, occupational, deployment, environmental, regulatory, and documentation-based risk factors by combining:

1. Examinee/applicant/employee medical history
2. Injury and surgical history
3. Current medications and treatment requirements
4. Essential job functions
5. Physical/cognitive/environmental job demands
6. Agency-specific standards
7. Deployment standards
8. Country-specific deployment risks
9. NFPA-related firefighter/first responder standards
10. FAA/aviation-related medical considerations
11. MOD deployment guidance
12. OSHA injury and illness data
13. Bureau of Labor Statistics occupational injury/fatality trends
14. CDC/NIOSH occupational health resources
15. Health disparity and socioeconomic access-to-care context
16. Documentation gaps and provider clarification needs

The app must generate structured SME risk intelligence reports, not final medical clearance determinations.

Critical safety rule:
The app must never independently state that an individual is qualified, disqualified, deployable, non-deployable, fit, unfit, safe, or unsafe.
Only a human SME may enter final determination language.

The app should use language such as:
- “potential risk factor”
- “requires SME review”
- “may warrant clarification”
- “based on available documentation”
- “available records indicate”
- “provider documentation states”
- “documentation is insufficient to determine”
- “risk may be increased depending on job duties and environmental conditions”
- “final interpretation requires SME review”

Build modular architecture so later versions can connect to:

Health Disparity / Access-to-Care Module:
Create a careful, non-discriminatory context module.

Important:
Socioeconomic background, race, ethnicity, poverty, neighborhood, education, or other protected/personal characteristics must NOT be used to penalize the individual or reduce employment opportunity.
This module should only be used to identify support needs, access barriers, documentation barriers, health equity considerations, and reasonable follow-up planning.

Allowed framing:
- access-to-care barrier
- transportation barrier
- medication affordability barrier
- limited local specialist access
- language/accessibility barrier
- health literacy support need
- unstable insurance or cost barrier
- rural/remote care access issue
- follow-up feasibility concern
- need for clearer provider instructions
- need for accommodation/support discussion

Not allowed:
- Do not assign higher risk solely because of poverty, race, ethnicity, immigration status, neighborhood, or socioeconomic class.
- Do not use socioeconomic factors as a proxy for medical risk.
- Do not use disparity context to disqualify.
- Do not generate biased conclusions.

Fields:
- Access-to-care concerns
- Transportation barriers
- Medication affordability concerns
- Insurance/access limitations
- Local provider availability
- Specialist access
- Pharmacy access
- Language/communication support
- Health literacy support
- Follow-up feasibility
- Documentation completion barriers
- Support recommendations

Output should say:
“These factors may affect documentation completeness, treatment continuity, or follow-up feasibility and should be considered as support needs rather than adverse medical findings.”

Risk Scoring Engine:
Create a transparent weighted scoring system that produces decision-support scores, not final determinations.

Risk score categories:
1. Medical Stability Risk
2. Functional Capacity Risk
3. Safety-Sensitive Duty Risk
4. Sudden Incapacitation Risk
5. Medication/Treatment Continuity Risk
6. Environmental/Deployment Risk
7. Emergency Access Risk
8. Occupational Injury/Reinjury Risk
9. Documentation Gap Risk
10. Guideline Conflict Risk
11. Follow-Up Feasibility / Support Need

Each category should score:
- 0 = no identified concern based on available documentation
- 1 = low concern
- 2 = moderate concern
- 3 = high concern
- U = unknown / insufficient documentation

For each score, require:
- supporting evidence
- source strength
- documentation confidence
- relevant job duty
- relevant guideline/source
- suggested SME question
- suggested follow-up documentation

Do not generate one simplistic total score only.
Instead generate:
- category scores
- overall risk profile
- documentation confidence score
- “SME review priority”
- “records needed before final review”

Suggested weighting:
- Sudden incapacitation risk: high weight for aviation, driving, weapons, security, emergency response
- Medication continuity: high weight for remote/deployment settings
- Environmental risk: high weight for extreme heat/cold/altitude/poor air quality
- Functional capacity: high weight for labor/firefighter/security roles
- Documentation gaps: high weight when condition stability is unclear
- Health disparity/access factors: should affect support planning, not adverse qualification scoring

Report wording:
Use neutral, evidence-based language.
Do not overstate.
Clearly separate:
- documented facts
- self-reported information
- guideline considerations
- algorithmic risk flags
- SME interpretation
- final SME recommendation

Example language:
“Available records indicate a history of [condition]. This may be occupationally relevant because the assigned role involves [job demand], and the selected guideline library notes [risk consideration]. The current documentation does not clearly address [missing information]. SME review is recommended to determine whether additional clarification is needed.”

For socioeconomic/access context:
“Access-to-care factors, including [barrier], may affect treatment continuity, documentation completeness, or follow-up feasibility. These factors should be considered as support needs and should not be interpreted as adverse medical findings.”

For scoring:
“The risk scores below are decision-support indicators based on available documentation, selected job demands, and selected guideline references. They are not final medical determinations.”
