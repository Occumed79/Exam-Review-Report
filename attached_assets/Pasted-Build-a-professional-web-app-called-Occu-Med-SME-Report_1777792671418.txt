Build a professional web app called “Occu-Med SME Report Builder.”

Purpose:
Create a secure occupational health report-drafting tool for Subject Matter Experts. The app should help organize an examinee/applicant/employee’s medical condition history, injury history, job duties, deployment or agency requirements, and relevant occupational medicine guidance into a structured SME review report.

Important:
This app must NOT make final medical clearance decisions on its own.
It must NOT diagnose.
It must NOT state that someone is qualified, disqualified, deployable, or non-deployable unless a human SME enters that conclusion.
The app should assist with report drafting, evidence organization, risk-factor identification, and guideline comparison for human review.

The app should use careful language such as:
- “potential risk factor”
- “requires SME review”
- “may warrant clarification”
- “based on available documentation”
- “available records indicate”
- “provider documentation states”
- “additional information may be needed”
- “the final recommendation must be made by the reviewing SME”

Design style:
Use a high-end professional occupational medicine dashboard look:
- MacOS Tahoe / Apple Liquid Glass inspired UI
- frosted translucent panels
- luminous moving background orbs
- glowing borders on hover/click
- dark navy, cyan, aqua, white glass, soft green and yellow accents
- clean rounded cards
- serious medical-documentation feel
- polished, calm, and organized
- no childish icons
- easy-to-scan sections
- professional enough for Occu-Med internal use

Tech requirements:
Use React + Vite + Tailwind CSS.
Use localStorage for version 1.
No paid APIs required for version 1.
Build the code modularly so Supabase, document upload, AI extraction, and secure authentication can be added later.
Include placeholder hooks/components where future AI extraction and guideline retrieval can be connected.

Core workflow:
1. Create or select a case
2. Enter examinee demographics and role details
3. Enter job title and essential job functions
4. Enter medical condition history
5. Enter injury history
6. Enter current medications/treatments
7. Enter provider documentation summaries or direct quotes
8. Select relevant standard/guideline category
9. Identify risk factors
10. Generate a structured SME report draft
11. Allow SME to edit final assessment and recommendation language
12. Export/copy/print the report

Risk Factor Analyzer:
This should not make final decisions.
It should compare case information against selected job duties and guideline entries, then organize possible risk factors.

Risk categories:
1. Functional risk factors
2. Safety-sensitive risk factors
3. Medication-related risk factors
4. Environmental/deployment risk factors
5. Recurrence or exacerbation risk factors
6. Emergency access risk factors
7. Documentation gaps
8. Accommodation/restriction considerations
9. Follow-up needed

For each risk factor, display:
- Risk factor name
- Supporting case information
- Relevant job duty
- Relevant guideline/source note
- Severity estimate:
  - low
  - moderate
  - high
  - unknown
- Confidence:
  - documented
  - partially documented
  - unclear
  - needs records
- Suggested SME review question
- Suggested documentation request

Example:
Risk factor: “Diabetes mellitus with unclear current control”
Supporting information: “A1c documented as ___ on ___.”
Job relevance: “May be relevant for safety-sensitive or remote deployment work depending on hypoglycemia risk, treatment regimen, and access to care.”
Documentation gap: “Current medication list, hypoglycemia history, provider statement, and recent A1c may be needed.”

SME Report Generator:
Generate a professional report draft with these sections:

1. Case Header
- Examinee name
- Case ID
- Job title
- Employer/client
- Exam type
- Reviewing SME
- Date prepared

2. Purpose of Review
Example language:
“The purpose of this review is to summarize the available medical information, identify potential occupational health risk factors, and assist the reviewing Subject Matter Expert in evaluating whether additional documentation, clarification, restrictions, accommodations, or further review may be needed.”

3. Documents Reviewed
- Medical history questionnaire
- Exam forms
- Provider letters
- Specialist notes
- Lab results
- Imaging
- Medication list
- Job description
- Agency/client standards
- Other documents

4. Relevant Medical History Summary
For each condition:
- Condition
- Date/history
- Current status
- Treatment
- Relevant provider statement
- Relevant test results
- Current symptoms/limitations if documented

Use careful wording:
“Available records indicate…”
“Provider documentation states…”
“The examinee reported…”
“Records reviewed do not clearly indicate…”

5. Relevant Injury / Surgical History Summary
Same structure:
- Injury/surgery
- Date
- Treatment
- Current status
- Residual limitations
- Functional relevance

6. Job Duties and Occupational Demands
Summarize:
- Physical demands
- Safety-sensitive demands
- Environmental/deployment demands
- Essential job functions
- Remote/austere work considerations if applicable

7. Applicable Guideline / Standard Considerations
Summarize selected guidelines:
- Source/agency
- Relevant condition category
- Key considerations
- Documentation usually needed
- Risk factors to consider

Important:
Do not state that a guideline automatically disqualifies someone unless the user manually enters that standard and the SME confirms it.

8. Potential Risk Factors Identified
Organize into:
- Functional limitations
- Safety-sensitive concerns
- Medication side effects
- Risk of exacerbation
- Remote/deployment risk
- Need for urgent/specialty care
- Documentation gaps
- Accommodation/restriction questions

9. Documentation Gaps / Clarification Needed
Generate a list of missing information, such as:
- Current diagnosis/status
- Medication list
- Treatment compliance
- Recent labs/testing
- Specialist clearance/comment
- Functional limitations
- Restrictions/accommodations
- Ability to perform essential job functions
- Stability of condition
- Risk of recurrence
- Need for follow-up or monitoring

10. Suggested Follow-Up Questions
Generate provider-facing questions:
- Please clarify the current diagnosis and stability.
- Please list current treatment and medications.
- Are there any restrictions or accommodations?
- Can the individual perform the essential job functions described?
- Is the condition likely to worsen in the work/deployment environment?
- Is urgent or specialty care likely to be required?
- Are there medication side effects that could impair safety-sensitive duties?
- Has the condition resulted in recent hospitalization, ER visits, or functional impairment?
- What monitoring or follow-up is recommended?

11. SME Assessment Section
This section should be editable and left for the SME.
Include fields:
- SME clinical interpretation
- Occupational relevance
- Risk level
- Documentation sufficiency
- Additional records needed
- Final SME recommendation

Recommendation options:
- No additional information needed
- Additional information requested
- Recommend delay pending clarification
- Recommend restriction/accommodation review
- Recommend client/employer review
- Recommend waiver/agency review if applicable
- Other

12. Final Report Language
Generate a polished professional narrative using the information entered.

Report tone:
- formal
- neutral
- evidence-based
- not accusatory
- not overly definitive
- based only on available documentation

Language Bank / Templates:
Include reusable language for common report sections.

Examples:

Opening:
“Available medical documentation was reviewed to assess whether the reported medical history may present occupational health considerations in relation to the essential job functions and applicable client or agency requirements.”

Risk-factor wording:
“The available records identify a history of [condition]. This may be occupationally relevant depending on current stability, treatment requirements, functional limitations, medication effects, and the demands of the assigned role.”

Documentation gap wording:
“The records provided do not clearly address [missing information]. Additional clarification from the treating provider may assist the reviewing SME in determining the occupational relevance of this condition.”

Provider quote handling:
Allow direct quotes from provider documentation and clearly label them:
“Provider documentation states: ‘[quote].’”

SME limitation wording:
“This draft is intended to assist SME review and does not independently determine qualification, disqualification, deployment eligibility, or employment status.”
